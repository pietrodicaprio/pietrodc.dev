I wrote the first line of code for this company in May 2025. I wrote the first invoice in March 2026. In between, ten months in which the bank account barely moved, the product pivoted three times, and I learned that «zero revenue» isn't a data point — it's an environment.

The environment changes how you think. Decisions weigh differently. It isn't anxiety, or not only — it's a kind of constant friction that forces you to ask, before anything else: does this actually bring me closer to a paying customer? Most of the time, if you're honest, the answer is no.

The most useful lesson: telling apart things that look like work from things that are work. Cleaning up a repo is work. Rewriting the pitch deck for the twentieth time usually isn't. Opening a new marketing channel is work. Tweaking the font on the website almost never is.

> Noise accumulates by default. Clarity is built by hand, one piece at a time.

At some point I stopped looking at monthly revenue and started looking at conversations with potential customers per month. From there the trajectory became legible — not faster, but legible. And that, a year earlier, it wasn't.

If you're going through the same season: time passes anyway. Better to spend it having talked to ten more people.
